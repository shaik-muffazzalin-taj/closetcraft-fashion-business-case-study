# ClosetCraft — Digital Wardrobe & Fashion Business Concept

**Your Wardrobe. Your Way.**

ClosetCraft is a business-plan case study exploring how a digital wardrobe could help people organise their clothes, plan outfits and make more considered purchasing decisions. It connects a proposed fashion app with customer needs, competitor analysis, commercial planning and a phased development roadmap.

**Project status:** Concept and planning presentation. The app, AI capabilities and commercial partnerships described here are proposed; this repository does not contain an implemented product.

## The problem and intended audience

The concept focuses on students and young professionals who enjoy fashion but may struggle to keep track of their wardrobe or decide what to wear. Its starting hypothesis is that greater visibility of existing clothing could make outfit planning easier and encourage people to reuse what they already own.

These customer needs are hypotheses explored in the presentation, rather than findings from customer interviews or user testing.

## Proposed product

| Capability | Intended customer value |
| --- | --- |
| Digital wardrobe with clothing uploads and tags | Make existing items easier to organise and find |
| Outfit planning and saved favourites | Reduce the effort of choosing and remembering combinations |
| AI-assisted recommendations using weather, occasion and style preferences | Suggest more relevant outfit combinations |
| Resale and donation options | Help customers consider alternatives for unused clothing |
| Wardrobe-use insights | Encourage more thoughtful use of existing items |

## Business and fashion analysis

The presentation brings together:

- **Customer segmentation:** An initial audience defined by lifestyle, interest in fashion and everyday wardrobe needs.
- **Competitor review:** A comparison of digital wardrobe concepts, including Whering and Smart Closet, to explore possible differentiation.
- **Commercial planning:** A proposed freemium model, a premium subscription assumption of €4.99 per month, and potential affiliate and brand-partnership revenue.
- **Marketing proposals:** Social platforms, creator collaborations, app-store discovery and referral incentives.
- **Financial planning:** Preliminary estimates for development, design, cloud services, marketing and contingency costs.

Pricing, costs and revenue streams are planning assumptions, not validated forecasts or evidence of actual sales. No brand partnerships or affiliate agreements are established by this case study.

## Proposed development roadmap

| Phase | Planned scope |
| --- | --- |
| 1 — Initial product | Wardrobe uploads, tagging, basic profiles and initial outfit recommendations |
| 2 — Personalisation | Style diary, favourites, weather-based recommendations and image tagging |
| 3 — Circular-fashion features | Resale or donation options, wardrobe-use tracking and potential partner stores |

The proposed implementation stack is React, Spring Boot and Python, with Figma for design and GitHub, Notion and Slack for collaboration.

## Risks and next steps

The case study identifies adoption, competition and privacy as key risks. Proposed responses include simpler onboarding, a clearer customer proposition and careful handling of wardrobe photographs.

Further work would include validating customer needs, updating the competitor review, testing pricing assumptions and assessing development costs. Any recommendation or sustainability feature would also need evaluation before making claims about its effectiveness or impact.

## What this case study demonstrates

The artifact provides evidence of business-concept development, structured presentation, customer segmentation, competitor comparison, preliminary financial planning and connecting customer needs with proposed product features. Its fashion and e-commerce relevance comes from exploring wardrobe behaviour, product discovery and potential commercial models.
